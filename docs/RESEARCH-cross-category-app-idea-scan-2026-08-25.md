# Cross-Category App and Website Opportunity Scan

**Research date:** 2026-08-25

**Objective:** find credible routes from the current $0 revenue position toward $5,000–$10,000 monthly income,
without assuming that Agent OS, QA, or any previously discussed product must be the answer.

## Executive answer

This is a broad decision scan, not a claim to have examined every app anyone could ever invent. It compares
representative products, prices, demand signals, and business-model constraints across 16 market families and
more than 60 specific product wedges.

The conclusion is:

1. **Near-term cash should still come from a narrow B2B outcome**, because five $999 customers are a more
   realistic first route to roughly $5,000 monthly revenue than acquiring and retaining hundreds or thousands
   of consumer subscribers.
2. **Local-service missed-lead recovery is the strongest new challenger** found in the broader scan. The offer
   is a bounded setup and managed reliability service, not a new general CRM or a generic voice-agent company.
3. **Nutrition is a valid consumer category to test**, because health and fitness apps have unusually strong
   subscription economics. The generic categories—calorie tracking, macro tracking, AI recipes, meal plans,
   and pantry inventory—already have strong incumbents and many cheap new entrants.
4. The nutrition wedge worth testing is **receipt-to-savings household meal execution**: turn the food already
   bought, household constraints, prices, and irregular schedules into the next three meals, while showing
   dollars and food saved. It must remain general wellness, avoid medical claims, and prove that people return
   weekly before a full product build.
5. **Career, certification study, renter/insurance evidence, scam protection, and caregiving** all address real
   pain. Each has a meaningful obstacle—episodic use, free substitutes, trust, privacy, regulation, or an
   incumbent distribution advantage—which makes it a probe rather than the primary income plan.
6. The correct use of the app factory is not to launch dozens of unsupported apps. It is to test two sharply
   different offers each week, kill weak ones using predetermined gates, and promote at most one validated
   product per month.

The expanded scan therefore changes the portfolio but not the immediate economic truth: sell a high-value
business result while running small consumer experiments. Do not bet the $20,000 runway on App Store discovery
or on trading.

## Scope and method

The scan covers:

- developer and AI operations;
- small-business revenue operations;
- health, fitness, and nutrition;
- caregiving and aging;
- education and professional certification;
- career and job search;
- personal finance and household administration;
- home, rental, insurance, and property evidence;
- travel and claims;
- shopping, returns, warranties, and consumer recovery;
- privacy, security, fraud, and scams;
- creators and media production;
- personal productivity;
- family, school, and community coordination;
- social, dating, games, and marketplaces;
- private investment research.

Each shortlisted idea is judged on the same weighted model:

- 25% speed to a paid pilot;
- 20% pain and willingness to pay;
- 15% reachable distribution;
- 15% fit with assets already built;
- 10% recurring-revenue potential;
- 10% differentiation and competitive pressure;
- 5% legal and operational simplicity.

The scores are judgments, not measured market facts. Desk research can eliminate weak ideas and expose
competition. It cannot prove demand. A deposit, retained user, repeat purchase, or referral is stronger
evidence than a market-size estimate.

## The market-wide facts that matter

RevenueCat's 2026 dataset covers more than 115,000 subscription apps, $16 billion in revenue, and more than a
billion transactions. It shows both the opportunity and the danger:

- new subscription app launches grew from roughly 2,000 per month in January 2022 to more than 14,700 in
  January 2026;
- only 17.3% of newly launched subscription apps reach $1,000 in monthly revenue within two years, and only
  4.6% reach $10,000;
- apps launched before 2020 still produce 69% of subscription revenue, while apps launched in 2025 or later
  produce only 3%;
- AI apps generate 41% more revenue per payer but churn 30% faster;
- the median North American day-35 download-to-paid conversion is 2.6%;
- Health & Fitness has the strongest category median at 2.9%, while Business is close at 2.6%;
- Health & Fitness leads early monetization at $0.48 revenue per install by day 14 and $0.66 by day 60;
- Business rises from $0.31 at day 14 to $0.50 at day 60;
- Travel and Health & Fitness lead median trial-to-paid conversion at 43.5% and 37.7%;
- 68% of Health & Fitness subscription revenue comes from annual plans.

Source: [RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-utilities).

These figures do not say consumer apps are bad. They say that code supply is exploding and distribution is the
scarce asset. A good product with no repeatable acquisition channel is not a business.

## Nutrition: the category deserves attention, but not the obvious product

### Why it is attractive

- Health & Fitness is RevenueCat's strongest category for day-35 conversion and early revenue per install.
- The category supports meaningful annual prices. MyFitnessPal lists $79.99/year for Premium and $99.99/year
  for Premium+; MacroFactor lists $71.99/year for one app and $89.99 for its nutrition/workout bundle.
- Food waste supplies a measurable consumer outcome. The EPA estimates wasted food costs $728 per US consumer
  per year, or $2,913 for a household of four.
- Nutrition and meal execution can be habitual, which is necessary for retention.

Sources: [MyFitnessPal pricing](https://frontend.myfitnesspal.com/premium?legacy=true),
[MacroFactor subscription pricing](https://help.macrofactorapp.com/en/articles/393-how-macrofactor-subscriptions-and-bundles-work), and
[EPA food-waste cost](https://www.epa.gov/land-research/estimating-cost-food-waste-american-consumers).

### Why a generic version loses

- MyFitnessPal already combines food logging, barcode and meal scanning, voice logging, custom macros, fasting,
  and a meal-planning tier.
- MacroFactor provides adaptive energy-expenditure and nutrition coaching.
- Samsung Food+ charges $6.99/month or $59.99/year and already offers 180,000+ recipes, AI-personalized recipes,
  seven-day meal plans, shopping lists, pantry automation, expiry prioritization, and nutrition targets.
- The Pantry charges $4.99/month or $39.99/year for receipt scans, inventory, recipe matching, family sharing,
  expiry alerts, health grades, and savings tallies. Multiple similar 2026 products are already live.

Sources: [Samsung Food+](https://samsungfood.com/food-plus/) and [The Pantry](https://thepantry.app/).

The rejected pitch is therefore: “AI meal plans, calorie tracking, pantry scans, and recipes.” That is a
feature bundle, not an underserved market position.

### The testable nutrition wedge

**Working hypothesis:** “For households with irregular schedules and food already in the house, choose the
next three meals everyone can actually eat and cook, use expiring groceries first, and show dollars saved.”

The first version should:

1. accept a grocery receipt, a pantry photo/list, household size, available cooking time, and non-medical food
   constraints;
2. return only three executable meals, not an aspirational seven-day plan;
3. let the household swap a meal in one tap without rebuilding everything;
4. create the smallest missing-items list;
5. ask what was actually cooked and what was discarded;
6. show verified weekly dollars used, wasted, and avoided—not imaginary savings;
7. avoid diagnosis, treatment, allergy guarantees, or disease-specific claims.

The FDA says software intended to maintain or encourage a healthy lifestyle unrelated to diagnosis, cure,
mitigation, prevention, or treatment is generally outside the medical-device definition; disease-related or
higher-risk claims can change that analysis. Source: [FDA Digital Health Policy Navigator](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/digital-health-center-excellence/step-3-software-function-intended-maintaining-or-encouraging-healthy-lifestyle).

### Nutrition validation gate

Do not begin with a native app. Run a concierge web/SMS test:

- recruit 20 households from one exact segment, such as shift workers with children;
- process their receipt/pantry and produce three meals manually assisted by the system;
- charge five of them $10 for a two-week test rather than relying only on free-user praise;
- pass only if at least 10 of 20 submit a second weekly check-in, at least 5 pay, and median self-reported food
  savings exceeds the price by at least 3×;
- reject or change the wedge if households will not repeatedly maintain enough input data to make it useful.

This is a retention test. A landing-page signup alone does not validate a weekly nutrition product.

The nutrition calculation is not a forecast; it applies the RevenueCat Health & Fitness median conversion to a
simple revenue target. A top-quartile product can do better, while weak retention or paid acquisition can make
it much worse. It explains why nutrition is a portfolio experiment rather than the plan for next month's rent.

## Recommended portfolio, not a single irreversible bet

### Cash engine

Run a real sales race between:

- **Agency Release Desk / AI Customer Journey Audit**; and
- **one-vertical missed-lead and quote recovery**.

Both can be sold before a new platform build. Give each 50 qualified contacts, the same time window, and a
priced proposal. The first deposit decides which receives the next delivery week. If both get deposits, keep
the one with better delivery margin and repeat potential.

### Consumer option engine

Run one small consumer retention experiment at a time. Start with receipt-to-savings meals because its outcome
can be measured in dollars and weekly reuse. A role-specific exam sprint is the next option only if an
underserved certification and reachable community are found.

No consumer probe receives a native app, broad feature set, or paid acquisition budget until some people pay
and return.

### Private capital-protection engine

Keep the investment product at cited research, strategy registration, backtesting, and paper trading. It may
improve decisions, but it is not counted as revenue and cannot be treated as a dependable $4,000 monthly draw.

## App-factory operating rule

Agent OS should make experiments cheap, not make abandoned production systems cheap.

For each new idea:

1. **Four-hour evidence memo:** exact user, painful event, current workaround, competitor prices, reachable
   channel, and bottom-up customer count.
2. **One-day offer probe:** one page, one sample outcome, one call to action, and a real price.
3. **Up to 100 qualified prospects or 20 consumer participants:** measure deposits, repeat use, and referrals.
4. **Concierge delivery:** use humans and existing tools to discover the repeated workflow.
5. **Product build only after repetition:** automate the part that at least three paying customers repeatedly
   need.

Portfolio limits:

- at most two active offer probes;
- at most two paid concierge pilots;
- at most one substantial product build;
- at most one new consumer retention experiment every two weeks;
- kill or pause an existing probe before adding another.

## What the broader scan changed

It did not find evidence that QA is the only viable idea. It found two meaningful additions:

- local-service missed-lead recovery is strong enough to compete directly with release assurance for the cash
  engine;
- nutrition is strong enough to earn a bounded consumer retention experiment.

It also prevented several attractive-looking mistakes:

- generic nutrition, pantry, resume, receipt, creator, finance, and AI-assistant products are crowded;
- the existence of a huge problem in caregiving or fraud does not automatically create a reachable paying
  customer;
- transaction products such as rental evidence can be worthwhile without pretending to be subscriptions;
- “we can build it fast” has almost no weight if distribution, trust, or recurrence is absent.

## Decision

The current rational sequence is:

1. ask agencies and one local-service vertical for deposits using two sharply worded offers;
2. simultaneously run the 20-household nutrition retention test without a native app;
3. allocate build time to whichever offer produces cash or repeated paid use;
4. retain the remaining ideas as a scored backlog, not as simultaneous businesses;
5. update the ranking with actual reply, call, deposit, delivery-cost, retention, and referral data.

The next discovery milestone is not another market report. It is the first paid evidence from these deliberately
different buyers.
