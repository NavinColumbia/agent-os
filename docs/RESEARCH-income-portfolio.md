# $20k → Durable Income: Founder Portfolio Decision Memo

**Decision date:** 2026-08-25
**Target:** first reach $4,000 in monthly recurring business revenue; preserve the option to compound toward
$200,000 over several years.
**Constraint:** the founder depends on this capital for survival. A strategy that can zero the account is not
an acceptable primary plan.

## Executive decision

Do **not** ask a $20,000 brokerage account to generate $4,000 every month. That is a 20% monthly withdrawal
before taxes and a 240% simple annual return. Turning $20,000 into $200,000 requires approximately 115.4%
annual compounding over three years or 58.5% over five years. Neither outcome can be made consistent or
nearly risk-free.

Use two separate engines:

1. **Income engine:** sell a narrow, high-value B2B outcome powered by Agent OS. Four $1,000 customers are a
   more plausible path to $4,000 MRR than extracting the same amount from a small investment account.
2. **Capital engine:** protect survival runway and build the private investment system as a research,
   backtest, and paper-trading tool. Live capital is not an income assumption.

The recommended first offer is **AI Release Assurance** for small software agencies and teams shipping
AI-built web applications. Sell it as a hands-on service before turning it into self-serve SaaS. Validate the
pet-care trust wedge and accounts-receivable wedge in parallel only as short customer-discovery experiments,
not as full builds.

## Reality and capital policy

The SEC warns that promises of high returns with little or no risk are a fraud signal; every investment has
risk. As a current low-risk benchmark, the 3-month US Treasury bill secondary-market rate was about 3.72% on
2026-08-17. At that annualized rate, $20,000 produces roughly $62 per month before tax—not $4,000.

Until actual essential monthly expenses are entered, use this rule:

- Ring-fence the larger of twelve months of essential expenses or the full amount needed for housing, food,
  health, tax, and immigration/legal obligations.
- Cap the first validation portfolio at **$900 total**: up to $300 for each of the top three experiments.
- Do not fund ads, a large data subscription, live automated trading, options, futures, margin, or leveraged
  ETFs from survival capital.
- A product earns a build budget only after a buyer gives a strong signal: a paid pilot, deposit, signed
  design-partner agreement with a price, or access to real workflow data.

## Scoring method

Each concept is scored from 1–10 on:

- **Urgency:** how painful and time-sensitive the problem is.
- **Willingness to pay:** whether the buyer can connect the product to revenue, cost, or risk.
- **Founder access:** how cheaply one founder can reach qualified buyers.
- **Speed:** how quickly the existing code can support a paid pilot.
- **Defensibility:** how much durable workflow/evidence data improves the product.

The score is a hypothesis to test, not proof of demand.

### 1. AI Release Assurance — recommended first offer

**Promise:** “Give us a staging URL and your user stories. Within one business day you receive reproducible
browser evidence, real defects separated from infrastructure noise, and an honest ship/no-ship verdict.”

**MVP:** a high-touch managed service using the Agent OS story explorer, browser evidence, defect repair loop,
and release verdict. The customer sees a concise report and evidence links; they do not need to operate Agent
OS. Start with web apps whose accounts can be seeded without regulated data.

**Why now:** AI test generation is already mainstream—GitHub reported that more than 98% of surveyed
organizations had experimented with AI-generated test cases. Existing vendors prove budget exists:
BrowserStack lists $175/month for one desktop/mobile parallel and $99/month for a five-user test-management
plan; QA Wolf sells managed end-to-end coverage, evidence, and maintenance through quote-based monthly
engagements. Checkly starts much lower for synthetic monitoring, which means the wedge cannot be “we run
Playwright.” It must be **requirements-to-verdict assurance with human-readable proof and defect resolution**.

**Fastest channel:** hand-pick 30 agencies from directories, LinkedIn, Product Hunt launches, and recently
funded/bootstrapped AI apps. Send each a short, permission-safe audit of its public signup or demo flow. Do not
spam a generic list.

**Moat:** story/evidence/fix lineage, infrastructure-versus-product diagnosis, release truth, and cross-run
learning. This becomes more valuable as AI-generated code increases release volume.

**Key risk:** Agent OS currently has a slow repeated-evidence-dispute loop. Sell only after a representative
three-story canary completes within a declared service window without weakening evidence semantics.

**≤$300 validation:** one landing page, three sample evidence reports, and 30 personalized outreaches. Offer a
$500 fixed-scope paid release audit credited toward a $1,000/month founding plan.

**GO in 14 days if:** at least 5 qualified calls and 2 paid audits, or 1 paid monthly pilot.
**NO-GO/PIVOT if:** fewer than 3 calls from 30 well-qualified, personalized contacts, or prospects consistently
say existing CI tests already give them sufficient release confidence.

### 2. Receivables Follow-up Autopilot

**Promise:** “Turn an aging report into consistent, relationship-safe follow-up, dispute tracking, and owner
escalations—without becoming a collection agency.”

**MVP:** read-only QuickBooks CSV import first; configurable reminder sequences; promised-payment tracking;
dispute/evidence checklist; owner approval before sensitive messages; weekly cash-risk summary. Do not move
money, extend credit, threaten debtors, or act as a debt collector.

**Why now:** Intuit’s January 2025 survey of 2,487 US small businesses found 56% were owed money from unpaid
invoices, averaging $17,500 among affected businesses, and 47% had invoices more than 30 days overdue. This is
an urgent, measurable pain, but QuickBooks and specialist receivables products are strong substitutes.

**Fastest channel:** one narrow segment whose invoice language is repetitive—small web/marketing agencies is
the best overlap with the release-assurance prospect list.

**Moat:** outcome history by customer relationship, dispute reason, message, and payment promise. The wedge is
not generic email generation; it is exception handling and owner-visible decisions.

**Key risk:** crowded integrations and legal/reputation risk if automation sends the wrong message.

**≤$300 validation:** interview 15 agency owners and manually turn five anonymized aging exports into a weekly
action brief. Ask for $199/month for an approval-required concierge pilot.

**GO in 14 days if:** 5 owners share an anonymized aging workflow and 3 pay or sign a priced pilot.
**NO-GO/PIVOT if:** prospects have fewer than five meaningful overdue invoices, will not share even anonymized
data, or only value fully autonomous debt collection.

### 3. Pet-Care Trust Ledger

**Promise:** “Keep every worker credential, insurance item, visit proof, safety exception, and owner-facing
trust record current—while your existing scheduler keeps scheduling.”

**MVP:** do **not** try to replace Time To Pet or Scout. Integrate/import their schedule and specialize in
credential expiry, sensitive-data-safe trust pages, signed visit evidence, incident escalation, and client
assurance. The existing dog-walking product supplies a meaningful prototype.

**Why now:** the Bureau of Labor Statistics projects animal-care and service employment to grow 11% from
2024–2034, much faster than average. The market is real but price-sensitive: Scout advertises an all-in-one
plan around $39/month, while Time To Pet sells established month-to-month operations software. A generic pet
scheduler will lose; risk/trust proof must save an owner from a concrete client, insurance, or staff problem.

**Fastest channel:** local pet-business associations, independent operator groups, and direct calls to teams
with 3–30 caregivers. Demo their own public trust page using fake data.

**Moat:** a longitudinal proof ledger and safe public/private disclosure rules—not bookings or chat.

**Key risk:** operators may like the trust story but refuse a second system or a $149 price.

**≤$300 validation:** show the current prototype to 20 operators; offer three founding pilots at $49 for month
one and $149/month thereafter, including manual onboarding.

**GO in 21 days if:** 3 deposits and at least 10 operators rank credential/incident proof among their top three
operational risks.
**NO-GO/PIVOT if:** fewer than 2 deposits or most prospects demand scheduling/payment parity before buying.

### 4. AI Client-Assurance Pack

**Promise:** continuously maintain the evidence an agency needs to answer client questions about AI use,
privacy, approval boundaries, vendors, incidents, and release controls.

**MVP:** policy templates are not the product. The product links each answer to current vendor inventory,
human-approval configuration, test results, incidents, and change history; it flags stale claims.

**Why now:** the US Chamber’s 2025 survey reported 58% of small businesses using AI, more than double 2023,
while 65% worried about a patchwork of state AI rules. This is a directional demand signal, not proof that a
small agency will buy another compliance platform.

**Price/channel:** $499/month; nine customers. Use the same agency list as offer #1 and sell a one-time $500
questionnaire/evidence sprint first.

**≤$300 validation:** ask 20 agencies for the last redacted client AI/security questionnaire they answered;
manually produce three evidence-linked packs.

**GO if:** 5 share a real questionnaire and 2 pay for the sprint.
**NO-GO if:** the work is rare, handled entirely by client counsel, or buyers only want generic templates.

### 5. Bid/No-Bid + Evidence Reuse for small government contractors

**Promise:** watch only relevant SAM.gov opportunities, explain qualification gaps, and reuse approved past
performance/evidence without inventing claims.

**MVP:** opportunity filter, deterministic eligibility matrix, evidence library, deadline/owner workflow, and
human approval for every submitted claim. Never auto-submit a bid.

**Price/channel:** $499/month; nine customers. Recruit through one NAICS-specific contractor community and
small-business procurement events.

**≤$300 validation:** manually screen one week of SAM.gov opportunities for 20 firms in one NAICS code and
sell a $250 weekly shortlist/evidence brief.

**GO if:** 3 firms pay and the shortlist saves at least two owner-hours each week.
**NO-GO if:** qualification data is too bespoke to reuse or firms will only pay after contract wins.

### 6. Narrow License/Permit Watch

**Promise:** one vertical, one jurisdiction set, no missed renewal, and an audit-ready proof packet.

**MVP:** verified regulatory source registry, renewal calendar, responsibility/approval workflow, evidence
attachments, and escalation. The product must show source provenance and “last checked,” not hallucinate law.

**Price/channel:** $399/month; eleven customers. Choose a vertical only after interviews reveal multiple
licenses and material penalties.

**≤$300 validation:** manually assemble renewal calendars for 15 multi-location operators in one city/state.

**GO if:** 3 pay for the manual watch and each tracks at least ten recurring obligations.
**NO-GO if:** obligations are too few, source data cannot be verified economically, or legal review makes the
service uneconomic.

### 7. Why not a generic idea tracker

It has weak urgency, low switching cost, low willingness to pay, and hundreds of free substitutes. It would
need about 267 customers at $15/month before churn, fees, support, or tax to reach $4,000 MRR. Agent OS can use
an idea tracker internally, but it should not be the first revenue bet.

## Ninety-day sequence

### Days 1–14: sell evidence, not software

- Make the current QA engine complete one representative canary quickly and truthfully.
- Create a buyer-facing sample report with no internal Agent OS complexity.
- Run the release-assurance experiment (30 qualified contacts).
- In the same interviews, ask about receivables and client-assurance pain; do not pitch all products at once.
- Run 20 pet-operator interviews using the existing dog prototype.

### Days 15–30: choose from money signals

- Continue only offers with a paid pilot or an equivalent priced commitment.
- Deliver the first pilots manually with Agent OS behind the scenes.
- Record time per delivery, model/API cost, failure rate, customer-visible value, and objections.
- Refuse custom work that cannot become a repeated workflow for the same buyer type.

### Days 31–60: automate the repeated bottleneck

- Productize the steps repeated across at least two paying customers.
- Add customer isolation, metering, concise progress updates, approval boundaries, and evidence retention.
- Price on the valuable outcome, with usage limits that protect gross margin.

### Days 61–90: reach a repeatable channel

- Target either four customers near $1,000/month or 8–11 customers near $399–$500/month.
- Publish anonymized before/after case studies only with permission.
- Automate personalized prospect research, but keep outbound communication human-approved until messaging
  quality and compliance are proven.
- Start a second product only when the first has a documented repeatable sales/delivery motion or fails its
  explicit no-go gate.

## Agent OS and Hermes architecture decision

Hermes v0.16.0 is installed locally and MIT-licensed. Its most relevant reusable patterns are stable cached
conversation prefixes, a narrow core with capabilities at the edges, session persistence/compression, skills
that improve from experience, and isolated delegated workers.

The live 2026-08-25 canary did **not** produce a model response: OpenRouter returned HTTP 402 for insufficient
credits. Its configured Parallel web-search free tier was also exhausted, and the configured Docker execution
backend failed its own environment probe. Therefore:

- Do not make Hermes a required Agent OS runtime dependency yet.
- Add it as an optional, governed worker only after a one-token canary succeeds.
- Pass a role-specific minimal toolset and bounded task dossier rather than the entire Agent OS history.
- Preserve Agent OS tenant, consent, budget, process ownership, checkpoint, and evidence gates around every
  invocation.
- Port architectural patterns where useful; do not merge Hermes’s large core loop wholesale.
